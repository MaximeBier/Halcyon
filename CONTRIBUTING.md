# Contributing to Halcyon

Thank you for taking an interest in Halcyon. This page explains how the
project is run, so nobody wastes an evening on a pull request that cannot be
merged.

## Halcyon is a one-person project

Halcyon is designed, written and maintained by a single author, who makes every
decision about what goes in and what stays out. It is not a community project,
and it is not looking to become one. The source is public so that you can read
what runs on your machine and check that nothing leaves it — see
[Does anything leave my machine?](README.md#does-anything-leave-my-machine).

## Issues are welcome

Bug reports, ideas, feature requests, questions about how something works:
please open an issue. Every one of them is read. When reporting a problem,
click the **⚙** at the top right of the page, open **Diagnostics** and click
**Copy log** — pasting that into the issue saves a round trip of questions.

Good issues describe what you were trying to do, what happened instead, and
what you expected. An idea does not need to be fully thought through; a rough
sketch of the use case is enough to start a conversation.

## Pull requests are not accepted

Code contributions are not merged, even small ones, even good ones. This is not
a judgement of your work. Keeping a single author means the licence can stay
simple: there is no contributor agreement to sign, no rights to track per
commit, and no ambiguity about who can grant a commercial licence. See
[LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md).

If you have found a fix, open an issue describing it — a diff pasted into the
issue is fine, and it may well land in the next release, written by the
maintainer. Pull requests opened on this repository are closed automatically
with a pointer back here.

## Running your own copy

You can run Halcyon yourself, change it, and share your changes, as long as you
are not offering a competing product. [docs/deploy.md](docs/deploy.md) explains
how to host it.
